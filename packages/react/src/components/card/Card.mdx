# Card

A surface that holds one thing's blocks together: an order, a figure, a section of settings, a
product. Its header names it, its content holds it, and its footer carries what to do with it.

<Canvas of={Stories.Playground} />
<Controls of={Stories.Playground} />

## When to use it

- **One thing at a glance,** such as an order's summary or the day's sales.
- **A section of a page,** such as a settings section, with a card inside it for each detail.
- **A product in a storefront's grid,** its picture to the edges.

Not for a thing's every field to edit: use a Form, in a card if you like. Not for rows of records:
use a Table.

## Import

```tsx
import { Card } from '@kaladan/diba-react'
```

## Anatomy

```tsx
<Card>
  <Card.Header>
    <Card.Title>Order 1041</Card.Title>
    <Card.Description>5 Nov, 18:20</Card.Description>
  </Card.Header>
  <Card.Content>{/* the order */}</Card.Content>
  <Card.Footer>
    <Button size="sm">Print label</Button>
  </Card.Footer>
</Card>
```

## Examples

### Surfaces

`default` is the page's card, on the surface. `secondary` is a card inside a card, on the second
surface, and `tertiary` a step further. `transparent` keeps a card's spacing with no surface at
all.

On either grey the muted text is a step darker, or lighter in dark, so a description or a note
keeps its contrast there. Everything in the card that is muted follows it.

<Canvas of={Stories.Variants} />

### Its parts

A header alone, content alone, or all three; leave out what it does not need, rather than leaving
a part empty, which would still take its 12.

<Canvas of={Stories.Parts} />

### Edge to edge

With no padding and `overflow: hidden`, a picture or a table reaches its edges and takes its
rounding; pad the words under it yourself.

<Canvas of={Stories.Flush} />

### As a link

A card that opens a page goes inside a link: the whole card is pressed, the hand shows over it,
and Enter opens it. Do not put buttons inside a linked card.

### The rest

`Card.Header`, `.Title`, `.Description`, `.Content` and `.Footer` take their element's props and a
`className`; `Card.Title` is an `h3` and `Card.Description` a `p`.

## Accessibility

A card is a group of what it holds, not a landmark: its title is a heading, so a screen reader can
jump to it. A linked card is one link, named by what it says or its `aria-label`.

## Styling

`.card` with `--default`, `--secondary`, `--tertiary` and `--transparent`; `.card__header`,
`__title`, `__description`, `__content` and `__footer`. Its surfaces are the theme's `--surface`,
`--surface-secondary` and `--surface-tertiary`, its shadow `--surface-shadow`. A grey card sets
`--muted` for what it holds from `--db-muted-secondary` or `--db-muted-tertiary`.

## As a block

A tree draws the same card, on the web and in Flutter: its title and description, a button in its
`action` slot at the header's end, its blocks, and its `footer`. `isFlush` takes its padding away,
and `href` makes the whole card a link; a card with an `href` holds nothing to press.
