# Button

An action: what the reader presses to do something on this page. A pill, its words 14 and medium,
in one of seven variants from the page's main action down to what destroys.

<Canvas of={Stories.Playground} />
<Controls of={Stories.Playground} />

## When to use it

- **To do something:** save, add, send, delete.
- **To submit or reset a form,** with `type`.

To go to another page, use a LinkButton or a Link: a button does, a link goes. To switch something
on and off, use a ToggleButton or a Switch.

## Import

```tsx
import { Button } from '@kaladan/diba-react'
```

## Anatomy

```tsx
<Button variant="primary" onPress={save}>
  <Plus aria-hidden />
  Add a product
</Button>
```

A Button is one part: its children are its icon and its words, 8 apart, in reading order.

## Examples

### Variants

One `primary` in a place, for what the page is for. `secondary` beside it, `tertiary` and `ghost`
for quieter actions, `outline` on a coloured ground. `danger` for what cannot be undone,
`danger-soft` for what is lost but can come back.

<Canvas of={Stories.Variants} />

### Sizes

`sm`, `md` and `lg`: 32, 36 and 40 tall from 768 wide. Under it, on a phone, each is a step
taller, 36, 40 and 44, a bigger target for a finger.

<Canvas of={Stories.Sizes} />

### With icons

An icon before the words says what the action is; after them, where it takes the reader. Lucide's
icons are sized for you. An arrow that points onward should point the way the page reads: left on
a Persian page.

<Canvas of={Stories.WithIcons} />

### Icon only

`isIconOnly` makes it a circle for an icon alone. Name it with `aria-label`, and put that name in a
Tooltip, so a mouse can read it too.

```tsx
<Tooltip delay={500}>
  <Button isIconOnly variant="outline" aria-label="Edit">
    <Pencil aria-hidden />
  </Button>
  <Tooltip.Content placement="top" showArrow>
    <Tooltip.Arrow />
    Edit
  </Tooltip.Content>
</Tooltip>
```

<Canvas of={Stories.IconOnly} />

### Disabled and pending

Disabled, it fades and cannot be pressed or reached. Pending, while its work is in flight, it
cannot be pressed but keeps its focus, and a screen reader hears that it is busy. Put a Spinner in
the icon's place, so the button's width does not jump.

## Accessibility

A button's words are its name. Give an icon-only button `aria-label`, and keep icons beside words
`aria-hidden`. A pending button stays focused, so the reader is not lost when it is done.

## Styling

`.button` with `.button--primary` to `--danger-soft`, `--sm`, `--md`, `--lg`, `--icon-only` and
`--full-width`; `[data-hovered]`, `[data-pressed]`, `[data-focus-visible]` and `[data-pending]`.
Its colours are `--button-bg`, `--button-bg-hover`, `--button-bg-pressed` and `--button-fg`.

## As a block

A tree draws the same button, on the web and in Flutter, with its icon by name before or after
its words. An arrow is named as for left-to-right, `chevron-right` for "next", and turned on a
Persian page for you. An icon-only button's `label` is its name and its tooltip, and a pending
one shows a spinner in its icon's place. Its `variant` is `secondary` and its `size` `sm` unless
the tree says otherwise.
