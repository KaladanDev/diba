# Pagination

The way through a long list a page at a time: back, the pages near this one and at the ends, and
on. Under the admin's orders, and under a storefront's search.

<Canvas of={Stories.Playground} />
<Controls of={Stories.Playground} />

## When to use it

- **A list too long for one page,** such as the orders, twenty at a time.
- **Results a reader moves through,** such as a storefront's search.

Not for a list read to its end in one go, such as a feed: load more as it scrolls instead. Not for
steps of one task: show which step it is.

## Import

```tsx
import { Pagination, pageItems } from '@kaladan/diba-react'
```

## Anatomy

```tsx
<Pagination aria-label="Order pages">
  <Pagination.Summary>1 to 20 of 234</Pagination.Summary>
  <Pagination.Content>
    <Pagination.Item>
      <Pagination.Previous isDisabled={page === 1} onPress={() => setPage(page - 1)}>
        <Pagination.PreviousIcon />
        Previous
      </Pagination.Previous>
    </Pagination.Item>
    {pageItems({ page, total }).map((item, i) =>
      item === 'ellipsis' ? (
        <Pagination.Item key={`gap-${i}`}>
          <Pagination.Ellipsis />
        </Pagination.Item>
      ) : (
        <Pagination.Item key={item}>
          <Pagination.Link isActive={item === page} onPress={() => setPage(item)}>
            {item}
          </Pagination.Link>
        </Pagination.Item>
      ),
    )}
    <Pagination.Item>
      <Pagination.Next isDisabled={page === total} onPress={() => setPage(page + 1)}>
        Next
        <Pagination.NextIcon />
      </Pagination.Next>
    </Pagination.Item>
  </Pagination.Content>
</Pagination>
```

It draws the pages it is given. `pageItems({ page, total, siblings, boundaries })` works out which
to show: the ends, the pages near this one, and a gap for the rest, keeping the row one width as
the reader moves through it.

## Examples

### Sizes

`size` is `sm`, `md` or `lg`: pages 28, 32 or 36 round from 768 wide, and a step larger on a
phone, for a finger.

<Canvas of={Stories.Sizes} />

### Many pages

The ends stay, with the pages either side of this one (`siblings`, one by default) and a gap for
the rest. A gap that would hide one page shows it instead.

<Canvas of={Stories.ManyPages} />

### With a summary

`Pagination.Summary` says which rows these are, at the start, and the pages go to the end. A
pager without one stands at the start; `style={{ justifyContent: 'center' }}` centres it, as under
a storefront's search.

<Canvas of={Stories.WithSummary} />

### Arrows alone, or pages alone

Back and on can show their arrows alone, each named with an `aria-label`; or leave them out.

It returns the row: each a page's number, or `'ellipsis'` for a gap.

### The rest

`Pagination.Previous` and `Pagination.Next` take `onPress`, `isDisabled` and an `aria-label` when
they show no words. Each part takes a `className`.

## Accessibility

It is a navigation landmark, named "Pagination" in the page's language unless an `aria-label`
says what it pages, such as "Order pages". The page shown is `aria-current="page"`. Tab moves
through its buttons.

## Styling

`.pagination` with `--sm`, `--md` and `--lg`; `.pagination__summary`, `__content`, `__item`,
`__link` (with `[data-active]` and `--nav` for back and on) and `__ellipsis`.

## As a block

A tree's pager works out its own row: its `total`, the page shown (`page`, or `defaultPage` for
one the merchant moves), `siblings` and `boundaries`, back and on with their words unless
`showControls` is off, and the page's digits. `onChange` is told the page chosen. On the web and
in Flutter alike.
