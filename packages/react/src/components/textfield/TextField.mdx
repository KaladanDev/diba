# TextField

One line of text with its name above it: a product's name, an email, a price. It holds its parts,
a label, the input or an input group with words or an icon either side, help under it, and what is
wrong in the help's place.

<Canvas of={Stories.Playground} />
<Controls of={Stories.Playground} />

## When to use it

- **One line the reader types:** a name, an address, a code, a price.
- **A value with a unit or a fixed part,** with an InputGroup: «تومان» after a price, `https://`
  before an address.

For several lines, use a TextArea; for a number stepped and formatted, a NumberField; for a search,
a SearchField.

## Import

```tsx
import { Description, FieldError, Input, InputGroup, Label, TextField } from '@kaladan/diba-react'
```

## Anatomy

```tsx
<TextField isRequired>
  <Label>Product name</Label>
  <Input placeholder="For example, Silk scarf" />
  <Description>As the buyer sees it.</Description>
  <FieldError />
</TextField>

<TextField>
  <Label>Price</Label>
  <InputGroup>
    <InputGroup.Prefix>…</InputGroup.Prefix>
    <InputGroup.Input dir="ltr" />
    <InputGroup.Suffix>toman</InputGroup.Suffix>
  </InputGroup>
</TextField>
```

## Examples

### Label, placeholder and help

A placeholder shows what to write and goes as the reader types: keep what they must know in the
label or the help. Without a visible label, name the field with `aria-label`.

<Canvas of={Stories.LabelAndHelp} />

### Types

`type` is the kind of text: `email`, `tel`, `url` or `password`. A phone shows the keyboard for
it; `inputMode` asks for one alone, `numeric` for digits.

<Canvas of={Stories.Types} />

### Direction of the text

A Persian page's field takes Latin text too. `dir="auto"` on the input turns it with the value's
first letter; `dir="ltr"` keeps an address, a phone or a code left to right whatever is typed.

<Canvas of={Stories.Direction} />

### Before and after the value

An InputGroup holds words, an icon or a button either side of the value, inside the field. Give
the group `dir="ltr"` for a Latin value, so `https://` stays before it on a Persian page.

<Canvas of={Stories.PrefixAndSuffix} />

### Validation

`validate` returns what is wrong, or nothing: shown at once with `validationBehavior="aria"`, after
the form is sent by default. `isInvalid` with your own `FieldError` words shows what the server
said. Left empty, `FieldError` says what the field's own checks found, in the page's language
whatever the browser's: a required field left empty, too few or too many letters as a reader
counts them, an email or a web address that is not one, text not in its `pattern`'s form. They are
a block's checks and words: a web address starts with `http://` or `https://`. Your `validate`
goes first. With `validationBehavior="aria"` the field makes none of its own checks: say what is
wrong with `validate`.

<Canvas of={Stories.Validation} />

### Disabled, read-only, on a grey surface, at its own width

In a column a field fills it. In a row it is as wide as its input, about 180, and `fullWidth`
gives it what the rest of the row leaves.

<Canvas of={Stories.States} />

### In a form

Its `name` sends the value. A required field left empty stops the form and says so under it.

<Canvas of={Stories.InAForm} />

## Accessibility

The field is named by its `Label`, described by its `Description`, and its `FieldError` is read
while it is invalid. Icons in a group are decorative, `aria-hidden`; a button in one needs its
name.

## Styling

`.textfield`; `.input` with `--primary`, `--secondary` and `--full-width`; `.input-group` with
`__input`, `__prefix` and `__suffix`; `[data-invalid]`, `[data-disabled]` and `[data-focus-visible]`.
The edge is `--field-shadow`, the fill `--field-background`.

## As a block

A tree draws the same field, on the web and in Flutter, with `prefix` and `suffix` words and `dir`
for a Latin value. Its own checks, required, email and url, are written in the page's language,
whatever the browser's.
