# Motion

Motion says what changed, never decorates. A control answers a press at once and settles quickly;
a colour eases over a quarter of a second; something that arrives slides in from where it comes
from. Every component carries its own motion, and all of it stops when the reader asks for less.

## What moves, and how long

Under each control is what its stylesheet moves and for how long, read from the page. A press
answers within 100 to 250ms, a bar fills over 300ms, a picture fades in over 400ms, and a drawer
slides in over 250ms. A switch moves as Flutter's does, by keyframes rather than a transition, so
it has no line here. A tooltip waits 500ms for the pointer to rest before it shows.

## The curves

Four curves do nearly everything, each a variable of the theme. Press Run to see them side by side.

| Curve              | Shape                                | Who uses it                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| `--ease-out`       | `cubic-bezier(0, 0, 0.2, 1)`         | most things: fills, ticks, colours |
| `--ease-smooth`    | `ease`                               | a button's press, fields, links    |
| `--ease-out-quart` | `cubic-bezier(0.165, 0.84, 0.44, 1)` | dialogs, drawers, menus            |
| `--ease-out-fluid` | `cubic-bezier(0.32, 0.72, 0, 1)`     | a tab's pill, a toast              |

## Less motion

When the reader's system asks for less motion, every component's transitions and animations stop,
and things simply appear. A page can ask for it too, for everything inside an element: set
`data-reduce-motion="true"` on it. `"false"` asks for motion even where the system would stop it.
Turn the switch below, and the bar's fill loses its transition: press Further to see it jump.
