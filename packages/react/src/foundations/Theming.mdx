# Theming

Every colour a component draws is a role (see Colours), and a role is a CSS variable. So a theme is
a few variables set on an element: everything inside it takes them, and nothing outside does. No
component needs a prop for it, and nothing is rebuilt.

## A brand over the gilt

A shop with a brand of its own sets the brand's roles: its fill, the words on it, its hover, its
soft tint and the ink on that tint. Diba's choices are drawn in their own variables, so a shop that
wants its checkboxes and switches in its colour too sets those, and the indicator a bar fills with.

```css
.shop-theme {
  --accent: oklch(0.6 0.11 190);
  --accent-foreground: oklch(0.99 0 0);
  --accent-hover: oklch(0.55 0.11 190);
  --accent-soft: oklch(0.95 0.03 190);
  --accent-soft-foreground: oklch(0.42 0.08 190);

  --db-control-fill: oklch(0.6 0.11 190);
  --db-control-on-fill: oklch(0.99 0 0);
  --db-indicator-accent: oklch(0.5 0.1 190);
}
```

The pairs matter more than the values. `--accent` is a fill chosen to hold `--accent-foreground`
at 4.5:1; an ink drawn on a surface is `--accent-soft-foreground` or a darker step, because a
colour picked to sit behind words is rarely legible as words.

## Light and dark

The provider's `theme` sets `data-theme` on its wrapper, and every role has a light and a dark
value. Nothing else changes: the same components, the same props.

<Canvas of={Stories.DarkMode} />

## A dark panel on a light page

A second provider sets its own theme for what is inside it. Give it `ownsDocument={false}`, so it
leaves the page's `<html>` alone; overlays opened inside it take its theme from the wrapper when its
`portalContainer` is `"self"`.
