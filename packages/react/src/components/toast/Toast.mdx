# Toast

A short word that something happened, at the edge of the page: saved, sent, failed. It goes by
itself after a few seconds and leaves the page as it was; a button in it can take the work back or
lead on.

## When to use it

- **What a button just did,** such as a product saved or a label printed.
- **Something done that can be taken back,** with an Undo in it, such as a product deleted.
- **How a piece of work went,** a spinner while it runs, then saved or not.

Not for something the reader must answer: use an AlertDialog. Not for something that stays true
while the page is open, such as a trial ending: use an Alert on the page. Not for an error in a
field: say it under the field.

## Import

```tsx
import { Toast, toast } from '@kaladan/diba-react'
```

## Showing one

A page has one `Toast.Provider`, the region its toasts stand in, once near its root. Anything on the
page then shows a toast by calling `toast`:

```tsx
;<Toast.Provider placement="bottom" />

toast.success('Product saved', { description: 'Buyers see it from now on.' })

toast('“Silk scarf” deleted', {
  actionProps: { children: 'Undo', onPress: restore },
  timeout: 8000,
})

toast.promise(save(), { loading: 'Saving…', success: 'Saved', error: 'Not saved' })
```

`toast()` is the default variant; `toast.info()` is the accent, and `toast.success()`,
`toast.warning()` and `toast.danger()` the rest. Each returns the toast's key, which
`toast.update()` and `toast.close()` take; `toast.clear()` closes them all.

## Its look

On the page's surface with the overlay's shadow, 24 round, 16 in across and 12 down: its mark at the
start, its title and a line under it, a button at the end, and a close button on its corner that
shows under the pointer or when the keyboard reaches it. From 640 wide it is at least 460 across, 16 from the window's edge; on a
narrower window it is the window's width less 16 each side. Up to 768 wide its button goes under its
words.

### How long it stays

Four seconds by default; `timeout` sets another, and `0` keeps it until it is closed. While the
pointer or the focus is on the region, or the page is hidden, no toast goes.

### Drawing one yourself

`Toast`, `Toast.Indicator`, `Toast.Content`, `Toast.Title`, `Toast.Description`,
`Toast.ActionButton` and `Toast.CloseButton` are the parts the provider draws each toast with; give
the provider a function as its child to lay them out another way.

## Accessibility

The region is a landmark, which F6 or Alt and T reach; a new toast is read out as it comes, without
taking the focus. Each toast's button and close button are reached with Tab once the focus is in the
region, and Escape folds a spread stack.

## Styling

`.toast-region` and its `--bottom`, `--bottom-start` to `--top-end`; `.toast` with `--default`,
`--accent`, `--success`, `--warning`, `--danger` and `[data-placement]`; `.toast__indicator`,
`__content`, `__title`, `__description`, `__action` and `__close-button`.

## As an action

A tree does not draw a toast as a block: it asks for one as an action, `{"do": "toast"}`, with a
`status`, a `title` and a `description`, on the web and in Flutter. An `actionLabel` with an
`onAction` gives it a button, such as Undo; `onAction` may not ask for another toast. A host that
draws its own toasts is handed the same words instead.
